\begin{abstract}
Interpretability work reports circles, helices and other low-dimensional shapes in neural activations, usually identified by eye or by fitting. We ask the topological question behind a common hypothesis: does a concept have a \emph{hole} you can travel around, and does its semantic cycle go around it exactly once? The motivation is manifold steering~\citep{wurgaft2026manifold}: if so, steering around the loop should be semantically meaningful.

We study the 44 top-3-PCA activation and weight clouds of a sibling shape-fitting project~\citep{shapefitting2026}, which come from GPT-2, Llama-3.1-8B, Pythia, a grokked modular-addition transformer and controls. We use two independent methods.
\textbf{(A)} The fitted shapes are uniquely parametrised, so their Betti numbers, Euler characteristic and genus are exact. A margin ratio $\rho_A$ measures whether the data resolve that topology.
\textbf{(B)} Alpha-complex persistent homology with design-matched Monte Carlo nulls, and circle-valued coordinates from persistent cohomology.

Results per dataset:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Colour hue:} a robust hole ($p={{exp2.colors.p_top_loop}}$, 999 null draws). Hue winds once around it, also in the top-10 PCs (post hoc), where ordinal concepts have loops too but do not wind.
  \item \textbf{Residues mod 113:} a hole ($p={{exp2.grok.p_top_loop}}$) that the residues wind once around. It is fragile under subsampling and fails the preregistered top-10-PC check.
  \item \textbf{Months:} a hole ($p={{exp2.months.p_top_loop}}$). The months go around it only in a post hoc analysis, and only in the top-3 PCs.
  \item \textbf{Days with times:} an unstable loop that the day does not go around.
  \item \textbf{Weekdays:} no detectable hole ($p={{exp2.weekdays.p_top_loop}}$). A corrected power simulation shows the preregistered test rarely detects 7-cluster rings. The days are ordered around their fitted helix's axis, but random smooth processes show the same ordering.
  \item \textbf{Ordinal concepts and 20 random-process controls:} no holes in the top-3 PCs. Ordinal concepts gain loops in 10 PCs, but their values do not wind around them.
\end{itemize}
Whether the fitter's ``helices'' for cyclic concepts are open or closed is unresolvable at the data's noise scale ($\rho_A \approx {{exp0.weekdays.rho:2}}$). Months come closest to closing.

The strict preregistered conjunctions pass for none of the three primary cyclic sets: {{exp3.H1A}}/3 by fitted-shape topology and {{exp3.H1B}}/3 by persistent homology. Specificity holds, but it is weakly informative, because sensitivity is zero.

On 39{,}000 synthetic clouds with exact ground-truth topology, the preregistered estimator recovers the exact Betti vector for {{exp1.P.acc:3}} of resolvable clouds (macro). A fixed threshold scores {{exp1.Ba.acc:3}} and the fitter's family label {{exp1.Bb.acc:3}}. Persistence beats the label only where the label is topologically ambiguous (open vs closed arcs, caps vs spheres).
Steering experiments are preregistered and postponed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Low-dimensional shapes in activations are a recurring finding.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Circles for weekdays and months~\citep{engels2025notall}.
  \item A helix of GPT-2 positions~\citep{yedidia2023helix}.
  \item Circles of residues in grokked modular addition~\citep{nanda2023progress}.
  \item Loops for colours and dates~\citep{modell2025origins}.
\end{itemize}
Manifold steering~\citep{wurgaft2026manifold,goodfire2026geometry} moves activations along a fitted manifold. On a weekday manifold this shifts the output from Monday to Tuesday and so on, whereas linear steering cuts across the manifold.

These works identify shapes geometrically. A geometric label such as ``helix'' does not say whether a concept has a \emph{hole}, a 1-cycle in homology, or whether the semantic order goes \emph{around} it. That is what would make steering around a loop meaningful.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The exact topology of every fitted shape: Betti numbers, $\chi$, genus, boundary components. A margin ratio $\rho_A$ tests resolvability. We also read out the intrinsic coordinates of every labelled point on its fitted shape.
  \item A preregistered, null-controlled persistent-homology test of whether cyclic concepts have a hole that their semantic order travels around once. It uses design-matched nulls, a projection-robustness check, and ordinal and random-init negative controls.
  \item A validation of persistent-homology Betti estimators against exact ground truth on 39{,}000 synthetic clouds of 13 families.
  \item Everything is reproducible from GitHub and Hugging Face, and every number is generated from results files.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Topology of neural representations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Layer-wise topology simplification~\citep{naitzat2020topology}.
  \item Zigzag persistence across LLM layers~\citep{gardinazzi2025persistent}.
  \item Betti numbers of modular-addition representations~\citep{moisescu2025modular} and H1 at grokking~\citep{tang2026grokking}. Neither uses a null model.
  \item A persistence ratio for small centroid clouds of imposed cycles~\citep{david2026context}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Neuroscience.} Ring and torus detection uses shuffle nulls and circular-coordinate decoding~\citep{chaudhuri2019intrinsic,gardner2022toroidal}. Circular coordinates themselves come from persistent cohomology~\citep{desilva2011circular,perea2023dreimac}.

\paragraph{Inference.} The universal null~\citep{bobrowski2023universal} gives per-bar $p$-values. Max-over-null Monte Carlo thresholds~\citep{gardner2022toroidal} are the practical standard.

\paragraph{Pitfalls.} PCA horseshoes and Lissajous loops~\citep{antognini2018pca,shinn2023phantom}, and Rips phantom classes~\citep{adamaszek2017rips}.

\paragraph{Gap.} We found no work that tests, with a null model, whether a cyclic concept's representation has a 1-cycle aligned with its semantic order. We also found none that compares the exact topology of fitted parametric shapes with persistent homology of the same clouds.

\section{Data}

The 44 clouds of~\citep{shapefitting2026} (commit 4c5f3b6):
\begin{itemize}
  \item Llama-3.1-8B hidden states: weekdays and months at the concept token, layer 5; days-with-times, colours, years, temperatures and ages at the last token, layer 19; city truth statements, layer 12; line-break character counts, layer 5.
  \item Pythia-410m character counts.
  \item Weight matrices: GPT-2 positional embeddings and the embedding of a grokked modular-addition transformer.
  \item Toy models of superposition~\citep{elhage2022toy}.
  \item Controls: 20 Gaussian-process and random-walk ordinal processes, and random-init networks.
\end{itemize}
Each cloud is normalised by the source's \texttt{fit\_frame}: centre, top-3 eigenvectors, skewness signs, RMS radius 1. Our numpy re-implementation reproduces the stored points to $10^{-4}$.

The synthetic test set (39{,}000 clouds, 13 families, 5--1024 points, 3 noise levels) and its validation stream are regenerated bit-exactly on CUDA, together with their ground-truth parameters (maximum difference 0.0).

\section{Method}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_pipeline.png}
\caption{Pipeline. Method A reads exact topology and point coordinates off the fitted shape; Method B measures persistent homology of the cloud. Both give a circle-valued coordinate for the travel-around-the-hole test. Steering is designed and postponed.}\label{fig:pipeline}\end{figure}

\paragraph{Method A.} Each family is a known space~\citep{hatcher2002algebraic}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item closed arc or ellipse: $S^1$;
  \item helix, line, ringing curve: contractible curves;
  \item disc, paraboloid, spherical cap: discs;
  \item closed sphere: $S^2$;
  \item cone frustum: an annulus;
  \item torus: $T^2$ (genus 1);
  \item clusters and polytopes: point sets.
\end{itemize}
The margin $m$ is the smallest parameter change that alters the topology (for example, a helix's endpoint gap). Its ratio to the noise-and-coverage scale $\nu$ is $\rho_A = m/(2\nu)$; the topology is resolvable if $\rho_A \ge 1$.

Every point gets the parameter of its nearest point on the fit: curve parameter, azimuth, height, or nearest centroid. The rules agree with persistent homology of dense noise-free samples for {{exp0.dense_agree:3}} of {{exp0.dense_n}} shapes.

\paragraph{Method B.} Alpha complexes in $\mathbb{R}^3$, which are exact \v{C}ech and free of Rips phantom classes~\citep{desilva2007coverage,adamaszek2017rips}, computed with GUDHI~\citep{gudhi}; Rips complexes with ripser~\citep{tralie2018ripser}.

A bar is significant if its persistence ratio $\pi = \text{death}/\text{birth}$ exceeds the 95th percentile of the maximum $\pi$ over 999 draws of a design-matched null. Every draw passes through the same normalisation.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Prompt-template designs use a cluster-centre null: concept means are redrawn from a Gaussian matched to the covariance of the means, and within-concept residuals are kept.
  \item One-point-per-value designs use a full-dimensional matched Gaussian.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Travel around the hole.} The hole coordinate $\theta$ is either the fitted shape's azimuth ($\theta_A$) or DREiMac's circular coordinate of the most persistent class ($\theta_B$; prime 47, at most 200 landmarks, \texttt{standard\_range}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item Concept circular means $\bar\theta_c$, with semantic phases $\phi_c = 2\pi c/n$.
  \item Alignment $R=\max_{s=\pm1}|\frac1n\sum_c e^{i(\bar\theta_c - s\phi_c)}|$, with a permutation null over concept labels (exact for $n=7$; $p_{\min}=1/360$).
  \item The winding number of the semantic cycle in $\theta$.
  \item For residues, the maximum over Fourier frequencies $k$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Preregistration.} The hypotheses, nulls and decision rules were pushed before any topology was computed. Amendment A2, which makes steering the primary hypothesis, was pushed before any persistent homology of real data. Deviations D1--D7 are logged with dates.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Instrument validation (synthetic, exact ground truth)}
Table~\ref{tab:exp1} reports the results. The preregistered estimator (alpha + universal null) is calibrated but conservative.
\begin{itemize}
  \item It is worse than a validation-tuned fixed threshold and than the family-implied baseline. The paired difference vs the family label is {{exp1.diff.Bb:3}} [{{exp1.diff.Bb.lo:3}}, {{exp1.diff.Bb.hi:3}}].
  \item Persistent homology is better exactly where the family label is topologically ambiguous: open vs closed arcs ({{exp1.Ba.arc:2}} vs {{exp1.Bb.arc:2}}) and caps vs full spheres ({{exp1.Ba.sphere:2}} vs {{exp1.Bb.sphere:2}}).
  \item On well-resolved clouds ($\rho_A \ge 4$; {{exp1.well.n}} clouds) the preregistered estimator scores {{exp1.well.P.acc:3}} [{{exp1.well.P.lo:3}}, {{exp1.well.P.hi:3}}] against {{exp1.well.Bb.acc:3}} for the family label (Fig.~\ref{fig:rho}).
  \item Tori and thin ellipses are its weak points.
  \item Outlier clouds (deviation D7: a point farther than {{exp1.out.factor:0}} median distances from the true shape) raise its false-positive rate on contractible shapes from {{exp1.out.P.clean:3}} to {{exp1.out.P.outlier:3}} [{{exp1.out.P.outlier.lo:3}}, {{exp1.out.P.outlier.hi:3}}]. This is below the preregistered {{exp1.out.thr:2}} threshold, so the DTM-filtration ablation was {{exp1.out.dtm}}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact topology of the fitted shapes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item {{exp0.n_hole_fits}} of 44 fits have a hole (cones for years and colours) and {{exp0.n_void_fits}} has a void (GPT-2 W$_\text{pos}$, a closed sphere). {{exp0.n_resolvable}} of 44 fitted topologies are resolvable.
  \item The three primary cyclic sets are helices with $\rho_A = {{exp0.weekdays.rho:2}}, {{exp0.months.rho:2}}, {{exp0.grok.rho:2}}$, so open vs closed is unresolvable. For prompt-template designs this is partly a property of the design (gaps between clusters). H1-A passes {{exp3.H1A}}/3.
  \item The concepts sit around the helix axis in order:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item weekdays: $R={{exp0.weekdays.azimuth.R:2}}$, exact $p={{exp0.weekdays.azimuth.p}}$, winding {{exp0.weekdays.azimuth.w}};
    \item months: $R={{exp0.months.azimuth.R:2}}$;
    \item residues: $R={{exp0.grok.azimuth.R:2}}$.
  \end{itemize}
  \item Position along the helix does not track the concept order (weekdays $\rho={{coords.weekdays.curve_parameter_t.day_of_week.rho:2}}$).
  \item This winding is not specific: {{exp0.gp_wind1}} of {{exp0.gp_helix_n}} Gaussian-process controls fitted as helices wind too, with the same minimum $p$. Only months stand out by their closure gap (Fig.~\ref{fig:helix}): {{exp0.months.azimuth.gap:2}} radii, against {{exp0.gp_gap_min:2}}--{{exp0.gp_gap_max:2}} for the controls. The weekdays' {{exp0.weekdays.azimuth.gap:2}} lies inside the control range.
  \item Post hoc, the months' axial position is a second Fourier harmonic of the month angle ($R^2={{coords.months.harmonic.h2:2}}$ vs {{coords.months.harmonic.h1:2}} for the first). The months lie on a closed saddle loop.
  \item Ordinal concepts lie along open curves (counting $\rho={{coords.counting.curve_parameter_s.value.rho:3}}$, temperature {{coords.temperature.curve_parameter_s.value.rho:3}}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Holes in the data and the cyclic tests}
Table~\ref{tab:real} and Figs.~\ref{fig:gallery}--\ref{fig:power} summarise the results.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Significant loops: colours, residues, months, days-with-times (unstable), and borderline the random-init grokking embedding ($p={{exp2.grokinit.p_top_loop}}$).
  \item After Benjamini--Hochberg over the 44 loop tests, the loops of {{exp2.loops_q05}} remain at $q<0.05$.
  \item Weekdays, all six ordinal concepts and all 20 random-process controls have no loop.
  \item H1-B passes {{exp3.H1B}}/3:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item weekdays fail at the loop step;
    \item months fail at the coordinate step (DREiMac's \texttt{standard\_range} requires a Rips death/birth ratio $\ge 2$);
    \item residues fail only the top-10-PC check.
  \end{itemize}
  \item H2 (specificity) is {{exp3.H2.B.falsified}}, but this is weakly informative: H4 balanced accuracy is {{exp3.H4.conj_B:2}} for every criterion, and the controls never approach the null threshold.
  \item Post hoc (\texttt{standard\_range=False}):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item months wind once around their loop in 3D ($R={{exp3ph.months.3d.R:2}}$), but not in the top-10 PCs;
    \item colours wind once in 3D and in the top-10 PCs ($R={{exp3ph.colors.top10.R:2}}$);
    \item days-with-times do not wind (winding {{exp3ph.daystime.3d.w}});
    \item non-cyclic sets with a winding record (any variable or dimension): {{exp3ph.noncyclic_wind1_ids}};
    \item random-init controls with a winding record: {{exp3ph.init_wind1_ids}}.
  \end{itemize}
  \item In a top-10-PC loop test with a matched null (post hoc), significant loops are found for {{exp5.top10.sig_ids}}. In 10D, loops are therefore not specific to cyclic concepts; only colours combine a loop with a single winding there.
  \item In a corrected power simulation (post hoc), a true weekday-like ring of 7 clusters passes the loop test in only {{power2.weekdays.loop:2}} of replicates, and a month-like ring in {{power2.months.loop:2}}. The full preregistered conjunction passes cluster rings in at most {{power2.months.ring_all_max:2}} of replicates, so H1-B was nearly untestable for weekdays and months.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Answering the question.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Topology finds a hole that the concept goes around for colour hue, robustly, and for residues mod 113.
  \item For months there is a hole, and the months go around it, but only post hoc and only in the top-3 PCs.
  \item There is no detectable hole for weekdays, with low power at 7 clusters. Days-with-times has a loop the day does not travel around.
  \item There are no holes for ordinal concepts or random controls. Because the strict tests never fired for a cyclic set either, this is weak evidence of specificity.
  \item The fitter's helices for cyclic concepts cannot be told apart from closed loops at this noise level.
  \item Whether steering \emph{around} these loops is semantically meaningful is the causal question. It is designed and preregistered (amendment A2), and postponed by the author. The strongest candidate is colour.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A convenience sample of 44 clouds, with claims made per dataset.
  \item Not blind: the source's labels and figures were known.
  \item The power for 7 clusters is bounded ($\pi \le 1/\sin(\pi/7)$).
  \item DREiMac's \texttt{standard\_range} criterion decided the months verdict.
  \item Exp4 bottleneck similarity was uninformative.
  \item The preregistered power simulation was misspecified (corrected post hoc, deviation D6).
  \item The alignment permutation $p$ measures ordering, not looping; only the winding number carries the cyclic claim.
  \item Synthetic accuracy for tori is near zero at these sample sizes.
  \item No causal evidence yet.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Persistent homology finds holes that the concept travels around for colour hue (robustly), residues mod 113 and, post hoc, months. It finds none for ordinal concepts or random controls, and it does not detect a hole for weekdays. Fitted ``helices'' for cyclic concepts are not resolvable as open curves. Steering around the detected loops is the next, preregistered experiment.
